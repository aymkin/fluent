# Chunks and paradigms (Dutch)

Load from `/fluent-vocab` and `/fluent-extensive`, and whenever an exercise is built around a word's
chunk. Evidence, limits and sources: `docs/research/2026-10-24-chunks-paradigms.md`.

A word is practised inside its **chunk**, first in a sentence that is true about the learner
(**self-reference**), then carried through other persons and tenses — its **paradigm** — one **cell** at
a time.

## Chunk

A chunk is the word with the words it habitually travels with: its article, its preposition, its object,
its partner verb — `boodschappen doen`, `wachten op`, `naar huis lopen`, `het eens zijn met`.

- **Pick** the collocation in the card's `Example` first; otherwise the most frequent pattern at A2;
  between equals, the one Russian builds differently.
- **Sense:** the chunk shows the first meaning in `Translation`.
- **Whole:** show and correct the chunk as one unit. The options of an exercise pair a word only with
  its real partners.
- **Non-calque** (`koffie zetten` — «варить кофе», `gelijk hebben` — «быть правым», `het koud hebben` —
  «мне холодно»): one line sets it against the Russian.
- **Sound pattern** (`kant en klaar`, `met man en macht`): name it.

## Self-reference

- A word's first task is one true sentence about the learner (`ik`) with its chunk: at most 6 words, one
  clause. Ask «что?», «где?» or «куда?»; a question about when brings a fronted time word and a second
  decision, word order.
- Facts about the learner come from the learner's own sentences.
- The third person is someone close whom the learner names.

## Paradigm by word class

Cells under "Ask" become exercises; "Show" goes into the paradigm table.

| Class | Ask | Show | Leave out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verb | jij question, u question, hij/zij, perfectum, `gaan` + infinitive | wij, jullie, zij (the infinitive); imperfectum outside the A2 set | — |
| Separable verb | as a verb, plus the split (`Ik bel je op`) and the participle (`opgebeld`) | `om op te bellen` | — |
| Reflexive verb | subject and pronoun (`ik … me`, `hij … zich`, `jullie … je`) | perfectum with hebben | future |
| Modal | presens (`hij kan`, `kun je?`), imperfectum `kon, moest, wilde, mocht` | — | perfectum (B1) |
| State verb (`kennen, weten, vinden, lijken`) | presens by person | imperfectum (`kende`, `vond`) | `gaan` future |
| Noun | possessive by person (`mijn, jouw, zijn, haar, ons/onze, jullie, hun`), plural | tense through the carrier verb | — |
| Adjective | after `zijn` by person; before a de, het or een noun | comparative | superlative by person |
| Adverb, preposition | tense through the carrier verb | — | person on the word itself |
| Pronoun | `elkaar` with plural subjects; `jouw` against `mijn, zijn, …` | — | `ik` with `elkaar` |
| Impersonal (`het lukt`, `gebeuren`) | the experiencer (`het lukt me/je/hem`) or the subject noun, and tense | — | person as subject |

Build a cell only where the form is standard Dutch and the learner could say it about their own life:
`hij wilt`, `gaat zijn` and `gezuld` get no cell.

## Cell

A cell rewrites the learner's corrected personal sentence for one person or one time marker. The frame
gives the subject and any fronted word, gaps mark the verb group, and a Russian line gives the situation.
One decision, as defined in `fluent-review` §3.

- `… jij ook brood bij de bakker?` → `Haal`
- `Gisteren … ik naar het station … .` → `ben … gelopen`

The frame's shape picks the tense: one gap — presens or imperfectum; two gaps — perfectum or `gaan` +
infinitive.

## Time markers and the A2 tense set

| Tense | Time marker in the Russian line | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| presens | «обычно», «каждый день» | `Ik … elke dag …` |
| perfectum | «вчера», «сегодня утром», «уже» | `Gisteren … ik … .` (two gaps) |
| `gaan` + infinitive | «завтра», «на выходных» (a plan) | `Morgen … ik … .` (two gaps) |
| imperfectum | «раньше», «в детстве» (a habit, a state) | `Vroeger … ik …` (one gap) |

Ask the imperfectum of `zijn, hebben` and the modals only: `was, had, kon, moest, wilde, mocht`. Other
verbs show it in the table, marked 👀, until a `/fluent-grammar` session has taught the imperfectum (its
session-log note names the rule). `zou` appears in set phrases (`Ik zou graag…`). The modal perfect
(`heeft moeten werken`) and the plusquamperfectum are B1: they stay out of cells and tables.

## Acceptance

These count as the same answer:

- `je` and `jij`, `we` and `wij`, `ze` and `zij`, unstressed `je` and `jouw`;
- `kun je` and `kan je`; `jij kunt` and `jij kan`; `u kunt` and `u kan`; `u hebt` and `u heeft`;
- `zal` + infinitive and `gaan` + infinitive.

`kun` stands only directly before `je`/`jij`. After «уже», the perfectum carries the result: `Ik ben al
naar huis gelopen`, while `Ik liep al naar huis` means «уже шёл».

## Grading traps

1. `werk jij?` drops the t; `werkt u?` and `wordt je moeder?` keep it.
2. `jij hebt`, `hij heeft`; `u bent` only.
3. `hij wil/kan/mag/zal` — no t.
4. zijn: `ik ben gegaan/gekomen/begonnen/gebleven/geworden/verhuisd`.
5. Motion: `ik heb gefietst`, but `ik ben naar huis gefietst`.
6. The participle ending follows the infinitive's stem: `verhuisd`, `geleefd`, `gezet`.
7. No extra ge-: `betaald`, `ontmoet`, `gebeurd`; separable: `opgebeld`, `ingevuld`.
8. `Ik bel je op` / `omdat ik je opbel` / `om op te bellen`.
9. `jullie vergissen je`; `u vergist zich` or `u vergist u`; `ik heb me vergist`.
10. `ons huis` / `onze kinderen`; `een mooi huis` / `het mooie huis` / `het huis is mooi`.
11. `groter dan`, `even groot als`.
12. `zij hebben`; `hun hebben` is an error.
13. `je hoeft niet te…` («не нужно») against `je moet niet…` («нельзя»); `ik heb het koud`.
14. `zal zijn` rather than `gaat zijn` (informal: build no cell on it, and leave it unmarked).
15. `wilde` is neutral, `wou` informal.

## Why

One «Почему…?» per round, after a correct answer, on the chunk least like Russian, where a real rule
answers it: zijn for a change of place or state, `op de fiets` against `in de auto`, `het` for every
diminutive. For a convention, say «так принято» and set it against the Russian: `Ik ga brood halen`,
while `Ik ga voor brood` means «выбираю хлеб».

## Interleave and return

- The next task on a word comes after a task on another word: the pause turns it into retrieval.
- Words alike in meaning go to different rounds.
- A round opens with free recall of the previous round's chunks; the missed ones are shown whole.
  Confusable pairs — jij and u questions, hebben and zijn — come back in later rounds.
